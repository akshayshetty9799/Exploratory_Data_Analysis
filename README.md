
# Analysis of TV Shows and Movies on Amazon Prime Video

## Project Overview

This project uses **Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)** to analyse Amazon Prime Video's content library and identify trends in content diversity, regional distribution, platform growth, and audience engagement.

The analysis uses two major datasets containing over **9,000 unique titles** and information such as title names, content type, release year, genres, production countries, IMDb ratings, and TMDB popularity scores.

Using Python-based data analysis and visualisation techniques, this project explores content distribution patterns and derives meaningful insights that can support data-driven decisions in the OTT streaming industry.

## Business Context

The Over-The-Top (OTT) streaming industry is highly competitive, with platforms continuously working to attract viewers, improve engagement, and retain subscribers.

Amazon Prime Video offers a large and diverse collection of movies and TV shows from different countries and genres. Understanding this content library manually can be challenging because of the volume of titles and the variety of audience engagement metrics.

Without proper data analysis, decisions related to content acquisition, regional investment, content recommendations, and audience engagement may not effectively match viewer preferences.

This project addresses these challenges by analysing Amazon Prime Video's content catalogue to uncover useful patterns and trends. The findings can help support content planning, improve recommendations, and guide strategic decisions.

## Problem Statement

The objective of this project is to perform Exploratory Data Analysis on Amazon Prime Video's content dataset to understand:

- The distribution of movies and TV shows.
- The most common genres and content categories.
- The geographical distribution of content by production country.
- Changes in content availability and release trends over time.
- The relationship between IMDb ratings, IMDb votes, and TMDB popularity.
- Patterns that can support content strategy and audience engagement.

The ultimate goal is to transform raw data into meaningful insights that support informed decision-making in the OTT streaming industry.

## Dataset Description

The project uses two datasets:

### 1. Titles Dataset

Contains information about movies and TV shows available in the catalogue, including attributes such as:

- Title
- Content type (Movie or TV Show)
- Release year
- Genre
- Production country
- IMDb score
- TMDB popularity

### 2. Credits Dataset

Contains information about people associated with the titles, such as cast members and directors.

**Dataset scale:** The titles dataset contains over 9,000 unique entries. The credits dataset provides additional information about cast and director relationships.

## Key Findings

The analysis highlights the following observations:

- **Movies dominate the catalogue:** Movies account for a larger share of the available titles than TV shows.
- **Growth in TV shows:** The number of TV shows shows an increasing trend from 2015 onward.
- **Leading content contributors:** The United States and India are among the leading countries contributing content.
- **Popular genres:** Drama, Comedy, Action, Thriller, and Romance are prominent genres in the catalogue.
- **Regional differences:** Countries differ in their content contributions and audience engagement patterns.
- **Ratings versus popularity:** High IMDb ratings do not always correspond to high TMDB popularity, demonstrating the value of analysing multiple engagement metrics.

These findings help illustrate how content distribution, regional diversity, and audience engagement can be evaluated through data analysis.
